// THE_BRIEF

Overview

The Challenge

BitesbySweden is a US-based Swedish candy brand selling guilt-free, non-GMO, no Red-40 candy mixes. Digitally Wrapped — a creative digital studio — challenged me to redesign two key pages of their website with a focus on Conversion Rate Optimization (CRO).

The Goal

The goal was not to make the website look better. The goal was to identify exactly why users weren't converting and design solutions grounded in user behavior and purchase psychology — every decision justified by a specific problem, not aesthetic preference.

The Constraint

Two pages. Two days. Desktop and mobile.

// THE_EVIDENCE

The Blueprint

THE AUDIT

Before opening Figma, I spent 30 minutes on the live site as a first-time visitor — experiencing it the way a potential customer would. I identified 8 conversion barriers:

Homepage:

  • Hero headline "The future of candy is better-for-you. And it's Swedish" — vague, no clear benefit
  • No prices visible on product grid — friction before even reaching PDP
  • No star ratings on product cards
  • Reviews section buried at the very bottom
  • "ZERO" section (no artificial flavors) — good concept but poor visual execution



Product Detail Page:

  • Yellow product image background — felt cheap, undermined premium positioning
  • $57.48 case price with no per-bag breakdown — sticker shock for US consumers
  • No trust signals near the Add to Cart button
  • No urgency elements
  • Accordion sections missing — ingredient-conscious buyers couldn't easily find what they needed

DESIGN DECISIONS

Benefit-First Hero Headline
Replaced "The future of candy is better-for-you. And it's Swedish" with "The Cleanest Candy on Earth. Experience Sweden's #1 Better-For-You Sweet Treats." The original headline described a category. The new headline makes a claim worth stopping for.

Pricing Transparency on Product Grid
Added visible prices and per-bag unit pricing ($4.79/bag) directly on homepage product cards. The original site showed no prices on the grid — requiring users to click through to a PDP before seeing any pricing, creating unnecessary friction and increasing bounce.

Price Anchoring on PDP
The $57.48 case price was causing sticker shock. I added "Just $4.79 per bag" adjacent to the price and reframed the case option as "Save 15%" compared to single bag price. Same number, completely different perception.

Trust Badges Near Add to Cart
Placed Free US Shipping, Secure Checkout, and Ships Today immediately below the Add to Cart button — at the exact moment of decision-making. This is where buyer hesitation is highest and where trust signals have the most impact.

Variety Pack Bundle — AOV Optimization
Used a high-contrast navy banner to create a visual anchor for the bundle offer. Applied price anchoring ($35.00 → $29.99, Save 15%) and social validation (1,200+ bundle buyers) to maximize Average Order Value. The dark background creates contrast that pulls the eye after the lighter sections above.

Cross-Sell — "Complete Your Mix"
Added a product recommendation section on the PDP to introduce customers to complementary products and increase cart totals. Positioned after the buy decision to avoid distracting from the primary conversion.

HOMEPAGE AND PRODUCT
DETAIL PAGE REDESIGN

The redesigned homepage follows a clear conversion
funnel: establish trust instantly → surface products with complete information → overcome objections → provide a bundle upsell → validate with social proof → capture emails for retention.

While the PDP redesign focused on three moments: the pricing decision, the purchase decision, and the post-purchase confidence. Every element above the fold supports one of these three moments.

// TAKEAWAYS

Reflection &
Final Thoughts

"BitesbySweden taught
me that the most
impactful design changes are almost never visual — they're informational."

What I Learned

This project changed how I think about ecommerce design. Before opening Figma, I spent 30 minutes on the live site as a first-time visitor — not as a designer, but as someone who might actually buy. That shift in perspective revealed problems I never would have found by just looking at the UI. Hiding prices on a product grid, burying reviews below the fold, showing a $57 number without context — these aren't aesthetic failures. They're information architecture failures that cost real conversions. The audit-first approach is now how I start every redesign: understand why it's broken before deciding how to fix it.

The Validation

This redesign was submitted as a design test for a UI/UX Designer position at Digitally Wrapped. Being selected to complete the assessment out of all applicants validated that my CRO thinking and design execution were competitive at an agency level. The two-day constraint proved I could deliver structured, documented, conversion-focused work under real deadline pressure — not just in a portfolio vacuum.

What I’m Most Proud Of

The design decisions document. Not the screens themselves — the reasoning behind them. Six documented decisions, each tied to a specific user behavior problem. That's the standard I want to hold myself to as a designer: never ship a design choice you can't explain. The BitesbySweden redesign proved to me that design thinking and conversion strategy aren't separate disciplines — when done right, they're the same thing.
